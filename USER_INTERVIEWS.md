# USER INTERVIEWS

Three quick conversations I had with developer friends and colleagues this week. Each took around 10-15 minutes.
---

## Interview 1

**Name:** Aniket
**Role:** Software Developer
**Company stage:** Large tech company (Globant)
**Date:** 2025-05-08
**Format:** WhatsApp call

**Notes:**
Aniket works at Globant which has built its own internal GPT tool for developers. He mostly uses that for day to day
work but switches to ChatGPT free tier when the internal tool falls short. I assumed he'd be tracking his AI spend but he had no idea - that's handled somewhere above him. He didn't even know what the company spends on AI tools monthly. When I explained what CostRadar does his reaction was genuine - "yes it's good to know how much we're spending." But then he shrugged and said it doesn't really affect him since the company pays. That's when it hit me - individual developers at large companies are the wrong audience. They never see the bill. The person who feels this pain is whoever approves the budget.

**3 direct quotes:**
1. "Yes it's good to know how much we are spending on AI tools"
2. "Company has its own GPT tool so I mostly use that"
3. "I use ChatGPT free sometimes when the internal tool is not enough"

**Most surprising thing they said:**
I had no idea large companies were building internal AI tools. I assumed everyone was on ChatGPT or Copilot. Globant built their own - which means the cost is completely invisible to developers like Aniket. He genuinely doesn't think about it because he never sees the number.

**What it changed about your design:**
Confirmed the target user is NOT individual developers at large companies. It's founders and engineering managers at startups who actually receive and approve the AI bill. Tightened the landing page copy to say "for startup teams" — not just "for developers."

## Interview 2

**Name:** Tejas
**Role:** Senior Developer
**Company stage:** Small analytics company (Reval Analytics)
**Date:** 2025-05-08
**Format:** In person - colleague

**Notes:**
Tejas is a colleague and senior developer at Reval Analytics. UnlikeGlobant, Reval doesn't provide any AI tools - everyone's on theirown. So Tejas sticks to free options - ChatGPT free, Groq, Cursor free tier. He's not overspending, he's actually underspending. When I showed him what CostRadar does he got excited in a way I didn't expect. He said if he knew exactly what was available and what it cost, he'd probably pay for something better. His exact reasoning - the right tool could increase his work velocity 2-3x, which more than justifies the cost. He wasn't thinking about saving money at all. He was thinking about getting more done.

**3 direct quotes:**
1. "Company doesn't provide anything so I just use whatever is free"
2. "If I knew what I was spending and what's available, I would buy something better"
3. "The right tool could increase my work velocity 2-3 times easily"

**Most surprising thing they said:**
I came in thinking everyone wants to spend less. Tejas wants to spend smarter. He's leaving serious productivity on the table by using free tools when a $20/month tool might 10x his output. That completely changed how I think about the "already optimal" results screen - it shouldn't just say "you're spending well." It should also surface tools they might be missing.

**What it changed about your design:**
Updated the "already optimal" results message to also show tools the user isn't using that could boost productivity. The tool isn't only about saving money - it's about spending it in the right place.

## Interview 3

**Name:** Bhushan
**Role:** Team Lead
**Company stage:** Product company (Accionlab)
**Date:** 2025-05-08
**Format:** In person conversation

**Notes:**
Bhushan is a team lead at Accionlab. He and his team use ChatGPT and Claude Code regularly. He was upfront - they spend a lot on Claude Code. His reasoning was simple: it's what everyone's using right now and the output quality is genuinely better. So they pay for it without questioning it much. When I explained CostRadar he immediately got it. He said if the tool surfaced a better or cheaper alternative with similar quality, they'd switch. But here's the thing - he already suspects they might be overspending. He just hasn't had time to look into it. That's the real problem. It's not that people don't care, it's that researching AI tool alternatives takes time that busy team leads don't have.

**3 direct quotes:**
1. "Claude Code is expensive but we use it because it's trending and the output is genuinely better"
2. "If we know any better option with similar quality we can save a lot on AI tools"
3. "We spend too much on AI tools but don't have time to research alternatives"

**Most surprising thing they said:**
Bhushan knows they're probably overspending but keeps going anyway because researching alternatives is too much work. Awareness isn't the problem - time is. CostRadar solves this exactly. It does the research in 60 seconds so they don't have to.

**What it changed about your design:**
This locked in the core value prop - busy team leads don't have time to research AI alternatives. The audit needs to be instant and the recommendations need to feel pre-researched and trustworthy. Also reinforced that "60 seconds" should be prominent on the landing page.
